Kletterfreunde Kreuzberg e.V. — a small Berlin climbing club, about ninety
members. Alex is its **Kassenwart** (volunteer treasurer), elected 2024. This is
not a business Alex runs; it is a set of books Alex keeps.

**These books do not go to a Steuerberater.** They go to the two elected
**Kassenprüfer**, who check them and report to the Mitgliederversammlung. There
is no VAT return, no EÜR and no Fischer — the deliverable is the annual
**Kassenbericht**: the year's income and expenditure, the closing balance, and
the receipts behind it, in German.

Closed **annually**, for the calendar year, in time for the
Mitgliederversammlung in **May**. So nothing is due here for most of the year —
the 2025 books closed in May 2026 and the next close is not till May 2027.

## What belongs to this set of books

Anything paid from the club account at **Bürgerbank Kreuzberg** or on the
**Vereinskarte** (the club's debit card, which Alex holds as treasurer). Two
instruments, one set of books.

The awkward case, and the only one that ever needs asking: Alex sometimes buys
club things on their **own** Meridian Everyday card — a rope, a competition
entry fee, a round of coffees after a session — and claims it back at the next
committee meeting. Those belong to the club even though no club instrument
touched them, and nothing but Alex knows that at the time.

Recurring: the hall block-booking at Kletterzentrum Ostkreuz (monthly, invoiced
to the club), Vereinshaftpflicht insurance (annual, February), the DAV section
levy, competition entry fees in the spring, and gear.

Membership dues come in over January and February by standing order, ninety-odd
small credits — they are the year's income and do not need matching
individually, only totalling.

## Where the material comes from each period

Bürgerbank exports the year's account statement as CSV from its online banking.
Invoices (hall, insurance) arrive by email to the club address. Gear and
competition receipts are photographed.
